About Me

I work on AI bias and fairness: benchmarking AI systems for minority and multilingual contexts, AI adaptability, and what AI means for the future of work.

Models are well-calibrated for the median, most-represented population.
The errors they make are absorbed by everyone else.

I want AI systems that cater to more than just the median user. That means real attention to under-represented communities and to languages other than English. I'm also drawn to what this technology is doing to work itself: who it displaces, who it helps, and how unevenly that lands across different groups. This mixed-methods, applied research background is what I want to bring to a PhD.

Outside of research, I make ceramics, have an eye for photography, and love trekking. Also very much a cat person.

Multilingual SDOH extraction from EHRs:
  • Designed and ran an evaluation of Llama-3.3-70B Instruct for extracting Social Determinants of Health from Brazilian Portuguese clinical notes, running inference on untranslated source text to preserve culturally specific phrasing and to keep inference local rather than exposing patient data to cloud APIs.
  • Built the prompt architecture: a five-category SDOH taxonomy with per-category definitions, few-shot exemplars, and a constrained output trigger; benchmarked against Llama-2-7B on CPU to document where smaller models break down on structured task specification.
  • Ran a two-annotator manual evaluation over a stratified 109-record sample, reaching κ = 0.70 (85.3% raw agreement); built an error taxonomy separating hallucination, prompt contamination, keyword anchoring, and missed implicit signals.
  • Key finding: the model is well-calibrated at detecting absence of SDOH signal (91–98%), but its positive predictions are near-uniformly unsupported, defaulting to “Education Access and Quality” in 67% of consensus-incorrect labels — indicating lexical fallback rather than contextual inference.
